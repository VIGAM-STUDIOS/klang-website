Three ways to find a sound

Describe it

Search in plain words: warm analog sub bass, bright metallic hat, ethereal shimmer pad. An AI model trained on sounds and their descriptions does the matching.

Say it

Click the mic and describe the sound out loud. Your voice is turned into text on your own computer, never sent to a server.

Drop a reference

Drag in any WAV, AIFF, FLAC or MP3 and KLANG finds the closest-sounding samples in your library. Or click Similar on any result.

Filter by feel

Narrow results by length (one-shot or loop), tone (dark to bright) and attack (punchy to soft), measured from the audio.

Preview and drag into your DAW

Arrow keys and spacebar to audition, scrub the waveform, then drag the file straight into Ableton Live, FL Studio, Reaper or Cubase.

Vital presets too

Search your .vital synth presets by description, and find presets that match the character of a sample.

  1. 1

Questions

Does KLANG upload my samples anywhere?

No. Analysis, search and voice transcription all run on your computer. Your audio files are never uploaded.

Do I need to be online?

Only once, the first time you launch KLANG, to download its AI models (about 430 MB). After that it works fully offline.

Is this a subscription?

No. Free is free forever, and Pro is a one-time payment that includes every 1.x update.

What happens when I reach 2,500 samples on Free?

Everything you've already indexed keeps working. To index more sounds or add more folders, upgrade to Pro.

Which file types does it support?

Your library: WAV, AIFF and FLAC samples, plus Vital (.vital) presets. Reference files for "find similar": WAV, AIFF, FLAC, MP3 and OGG.

Which DAWs does it work with?

Any DAW that accepts audio files dragged in from a folder, including Ableton Live, FL Studio, Reaper, Cubase, Studio One and Bitwig.

How does it understand what a sound is like?

KLANG uses an AI model trained on pairs of sounds and text descriptions, so words and audio can be compared directly. It also measures each sound's length, brightness, attack and loudness, which power the filters.
